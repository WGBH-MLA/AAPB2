# The Nebraska Heritage Collection

## Thumbnail

## Summary 

The Nebraska Heritage Collection features programming from The Heritage Library, a collection of over seven hundred programs produced for Nebraska public television between 1959 and 2008. The collection was curated by the late Ron Hull, former general manager of KUON and a pioneer in the history of public broadcasting. Hull selected the programs in this collection based on their historic value and artistic merit, preserving them for posterity at a time when programs were routinely recorded over or discarded.

Several programs in the collection delve into the history of Nebraska and the Great Plains region. One of the earliest of these was [*Land of Their Own*](https://americanarchive.org/catalog?f%5Baccess_types%5D%5B%5D=digitized&f%5Bseries_titles%5D%5B%5D=Land+of+Their+Own&sort=episode_number_sort+asc) (1961), a nationally distributed three-part series about pioneer life in the Great Plains region. Other historical programs include [*In Search of the Oregon Trail*](https://americanarchive.org/catalog/cpb-aacip-85cd582f11e) (2001), telling the real story of migration along the legendary wagon route, and [*Frontier University Dreams*](https://americanarchive.org/catalog/cpb-aacip-50835355064) (2005). Narrated by Nebraska native Dick Cavett, Frontier University Dreams recounts the history of the University of Nebraska through entertaining stories of the "botanists, bodysnatchers, and Bugeaters" who shaped its early years. 

The Nebraska Heritage Collection also documents the stories and cultures of Indigenous peoples and communities in the Great Plains. Noteworthy items include interviews with elders from Nebraska tribes conducted between 1977 and 1979 as part of the [Junior League Indian Language Project](https://americanarchive.org/catalog?f%5Baccess_types%5D%5B%5D=online&f%5Bseries_titles%5D%5B%5D=Junior+League+Indian+Language+Project+Final&sort=episode_number_sort+asc). Among the interview subjects is [Truman Dailey](https://americanarchive.org/catalog/cpb-aacip-73a311fd41a), the last native speaker of the Otoe-Missouria dialect of Chiwere (Baxoje-Jiwere-Nyut'achi). Dailey and other elders discuss migration and displacement, hunting practices, Native languages and cultures, and tribal traditions. 

Other programs in the collection highlight the importance of farming and agriculture to the region and the local traditions that make Nebraska unique. [*Backyard Farmer*](https://americanarchive.org/catalog/cpb-aacip-b16a9515d64) (1952–present), the longest-running locally produced television show in the country, is included, as is the 1982 Ak-Sar-Ben Coronation, an annual pageant established in 1895. In 1982, the Knights of Ak-Sar-Ben (that’s “Nebraska” spelled backwards) staged a big-budget musical spectacular culminating in the crowning of the 88th Ak-Sar-Ben King and Queen. 

The Nebraska Heritage Collection showcases a wealth of unique literary and arts programming, including a [seven-part series on creative writing](https://americanarchive.org/catalog?f%5Baccess_types%5D%5B%5D=online&f%5Bseries_titles%5D%5B%5D=Mari+Sandoz+Discusses+Creative+Writing&sort=episode_number_sort+asc) produced by Hull and presented by Mari Sandoz, one of the foremost chroniclers of the American West. [*Anyone for Tennyson?*](https://americanarchive.org/catalog?f%5Baccess_types%5D%5B%5D=digitized&f%5Bseries_titles%5D%5B%5D=Anyone+for+Tennyson%3F&sort=episode_number_sort+asc) (1976–1978) was a celebrated series of poetry-in-performance programs featuring famous guest stars, including Henry Fonda, Vincent Price, William Shatner, and Irene Worth. [*Is It Art?*](https://americanarchive.org/catalog/cpb-aacip-8230ce546e4) (1992) takes viewers behind the scenes into artists’ studios, museums, and galleries, while [*A Sense of Place*](https://americanarchive.org/catalog/cpb-aacip-293b7387daa) (1980) explores artists’ relationship to the American landscape.

The collection also includes profiles of artists and public figures, such as [*A Portrait of Jamie*](https://americanarchive.org/catalog/cpb-aacip-930980074cc) (1977), profiling realist painter Jamie Wyeth, and [*A View from the Standpipe*](https://americanarchive.org/catalog/cpb-aacip-273c06f9f3b) (1982), about Nebraska native John Falter, whom Norman Rockwell deemed “America’s most gifted illustrator.” Ernie Chambers—the first African American in Nebraska to run for U.S. Senate and the only African American to run for governor in the state’s history—is the subject of [*Ernie Chambers: Still Militant After All These Years*](https://americanarchive.org/catalog/cpb-aacip-a7f3eccfa89) (1997). 

Spanning almost fifty years, The Nebraska Heritage Collection brings together many of Nebraska public television’s most acclaimed, unique, and historically noteworthy programs.


## Background

For nearly seventy years, Ron Hull helped shape public media, both in Nebraska and throughout the country. In 1955, he joined Nebraska educational TV station KUON as a producer and director, where he produced series with author Mari Sandoz and poet John Neihardt, two of the nation’s most celebrated writers. In 1971, he successfully lobbied the Nebraska State Legislature for funding to build the Nebraska Educational Telecommunications Center, a state-of-the-art facility where some of the station’s most renowned series were later recorded. 

Hull was instrumental to the development of public broadcasting beyond Nebraska as well—during his tenure at the Corporation for Public Broadcasting, he helped secure funding and support for [*NOVA*](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/) (1974–present) and [*American Experience*](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/) (1988–present), and he provided seed money to documentary filmmaker Ken Burns for [*The Civil War*](https://www.pbs.org/kenburns/the-civil-war) (1990). Hull was also a founding board member and treasurer of what is today known as Vision Maker Media, the nation’s premier source for public media by and about Native Americans and Alaska Natives.

The Nebraska Heritage Collection features television programming selected by Hull and produced by [Nebraska Public Media](https://nebraskapublicmedia.org/en/watch/), a statewide network of public radio and television stations. Its flagship public TV station began broadcasting under the KUON-TV call sign on November 1, 1954. Three decades later, on October 19, 1989, the University of Nebraska–Lincoln launched the Nebraska Public Radio Network. Today, Nebraska Public Media broadcasts across five television stations and two radio channels, reaching audiences statewide. 

The Nebraska Heritage Collection was added to the AAPB in November 2026.
