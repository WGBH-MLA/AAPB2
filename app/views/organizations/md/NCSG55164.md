## About

Lionel Legros is a retired bilingual middle school science teacher, activist, radio broadcaster, Haitian art collector and consultant, Kreyòl writing enthusiast, poet, public speaker, adjunct instructor, and grassroots organizer who was born in Haiti with geographical ties to Port-au-Prince, Gonaives, and La-Valle-Jacmel. He migrated to New York City in 1965 and started off his immigration life like other working-class Haitians. While studying English at Columbia and working in Butler Library he took advantage of the University’s employee program and completed his B.A. in African History and he went on to study at Columbia University Teacher’s College and complete a M.A. in Bilingual Education from Long Island University (LIU).

From 1969 through its existence, Lionel hosted the award-winning and pioneering radio program, “L’Heure Haïtienne” where he served as an initiator, researcher, designer, producer, and sound engineer, and commentor which helped inform and galvanize support for humanitarian and political issues during a crucial time of struggle for student and civil rights. He has been featured and has supported film projects such as *Stones in the Sun* (dir., Benoit, 2012), *Flash of the Spirit* (dir., Karen Kramer 1985), *Haitian Corner*, (dir., Peck, 1985) *Forgotten Occupation: Jim Crow Goes to Haiti* (dir., Martin, 2023).

In 1972 he worked extensively with the Haitian Fathers of Brooklyn to assist Haitian refugees who were seeking asylum in the US. He devoted much energy to the creation of the Haitian Information and Documentation Center SELA (Sant Enfomasyon Lè Ayisyen) from 1982 – 2002 with a team of progressive Haitians. It provided resources like job postings, articles, books, and it was a a place where members of the community could meet to discuss historical and current events and organize forums to be better informed. He has done many lectures and has researched different aspects of Haitian culture.

He has worked for decades supporting Haitians in the Dominican Republic that have endured discrimination, denationalization, xenophobia, anti-blackness, and anti-Haitianism in the nation and in the US diaspora. He has traveled there many times for service work. He has been given multiple service awards including one from Movement of Dominican Haitian Women from Sonia Pierre (his proudest achievement). Legros has been interviewed by multiple news outlets like Democracy Now, WKCR, WBAI, and more. He is regularly interviewed by others about the history of the New York Haitian community. He strongly believes in service to the people and strives to live a life dedicated to justice, democracy, freedom of speech, education, and journalism.  

His daughter, Ayanna Legros, became the preservationist and custodian of the L’Heure Haïtienne/Lè Ayisyen archival collection between 2016 and 2026.
