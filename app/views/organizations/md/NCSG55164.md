## About

Lionel Legros is a retired bilingual middle school science teacher, activist, radio broadcaster, Kreyòl writing enthusiast, poet, public speaker, adjunct instructor, grassroots organizer, and collector and consultant of Haitian art. He was born in Haiti and has geographical ties to Pòtoprens (Port-au-Prince), Gonayiv (Gonaives), and Lavale Jakmèl (La-Valle-Jacmel). Lionel arrived in New York City in 1966. Like many working-class Haitian immigrants, he began his new life working in a Brooklyn factory. He went on to study English at Columbia University while working at Butler Library. Through the university's employee program, he earned a B.A. in History with a minor in African Studies from the School of General Studies. He later earned an M.A. in Bilingual Education from Long Island University.

From 1969 until 2000 Lionel hosted the award-winning and pioneering radio program, “L’Heure Haïtienne” (Lè Ayisyen) where he served as a creator, researcher, designer, producer, and sound engineer, and commentor. At a crucial time in the struggle for student and civil rights, the program kept the community informed and rallied support for humanitarian and political causes. His work has also reached film screens and museums. He appeared in and supported the films *Legacy of the Spirits* (Karen Kramer, 1985), *Haitian Corner* (Raoul Peck, 1987), *Woch Nan Solèy / Stones in the Sun* (Patricia Benoit, 2012), and *Forgotten Occupation: Jim Crow Goes to Haiti* (Alain Martin, 2026). He has also contributed to exhibitions and educational programs at the Brooklyn Museum (Haitian Art, 1978) and the American Museum of Natural History (Sacred Arts of Haitian Vodou, 1998).

Beginning in 1972 he worked closely with the Haitian Fathers in Brooklyn to assist Haitian refugees who were seeking asylum in the US. From 1982 to 2002, he worked with a team of progressive Haitians to build and sustain the Haitian Information and Documentation Center (SELA - Sant Enfòmasyon Lè Ayisyen). SELA provided resources like job postings, articles, books. It was also a gathering place where community members discussed and debated history and organized public forums to be better informed about current events. Lionel has lectured widely, researched many aspects of Haitian culture, and written for newspapers including the Guardian.

For decades, Lionel has stood with Haitians and Dominicans of Haitian descent who face discrimination, denationalization, xenophobia, anti-Blackness, and anti-Haitianism. He has traveled there many times to do service work in bateyes which are company towns of sugar plantations and mills in the Dominican Republic. He has been given multiple service awards including one from Movimiento de Mujeres Dominico-Haitianas (*Movement of Dominican Haitian Women*) from Sonia Pierre (his proudest achievement).

Lionel has been interviewed by Democracy Now!, WKCR, WBAI, and many other outlets. He remains a sought-after voice on the history of New York's Haitian community. Above all, he believes in service to the people and has dedicated his life to justice, democracy, freedom of speech, education, and journalism.
His daughter, Ayanna Legros, served as preservationist and custodian of the L'Heure Haïtienne / Lè Ayisyen archival collection from 2016 to 2026.
