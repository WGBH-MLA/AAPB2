## Introduction

In 1990 Maryland Public Television aired After the Warming, a documentary with a novel premise. The conceit is that the documentary is created in 2050, looking back to understand how humanity ultimately developed political and technological strategies to solve the problem of climate change. In this excerpt, narrator James Burke explains the carbon tax and cap and trade strategy that was supposedly brokered by the United Nations around 2000 to prevent global warming (remember, the 1990 documentary was speculating about the future). After the Warming acknowledges the difficulty of countries mustering the political will to arrive at such an agreement. The film explains that it took an ecological crisis—a series of droughts in the late 1990s that led to widespread starvation and political unrest—to force the issue.
Real history would play out differently, as the global treaties hammered out at international summits in the 21st century proved to be less impactful (and less binding on participants) than the one imagined in the documentary. Nevertheless, After the Warming provides a fascinating insight into the strategies and hopes of climate change reformers of the late 20th century.
