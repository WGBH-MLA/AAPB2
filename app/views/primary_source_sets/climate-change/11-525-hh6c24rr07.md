## Introduction

The most significant successor agreement to the Kyoto Protocol (discussed in a previous clip) was negotiated at the Paris Climate Conference in 2015. This PBS NewsHour report summarizes the key components  of the Paris Accords, and then features two panelists who discuss the efficacy of the agreement: Fred Krupp, president of the Environmental Defense Fund, and Robert Bryce, a senior fellow at the Manhattan Institute. Supporters like Krupp argued that the pledges made at the conference created a productive framework for future change, in which each country set emissions reduction goals but were granted the freedom to determine the best way to achieve them. However, skeptics argued that the targets were neither legally binding nor sufficiently ambitious, and the realization of the goals would be subject to the whims of domestic politics in participating countries. Indeed, when Donald Trump succeeded Barack Obama in 2017, he withdrew the United States from the Paris Agreement.
