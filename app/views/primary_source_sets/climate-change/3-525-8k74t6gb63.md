## Introduction

As global temperatures increase and ice caps melt, global sea levels have risen and will continue to rise, and the social impact on human populations could be enormous. According to a 2024 study [published in Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-73287-x), over a billion people live within 10 kilometers of the coast. Thus, many scientists worry that coastal flooding (along with other climate change-induced factors like droughts and storms) [could create millions of climate refugees displaced from their communities}(https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/environmental-refugee/). The effects of sea level rise are already being felt in many low-lying coastal cities, including the U.S. city of Miami, Florida. This 2018 PBS NewsHour report, which features footage from Miami and interviews with area experts, explains how the city may be a “future Atlantis.” But even in the near term, rising sea levels contribute to water contamination and floods that could overwhelm the city’s sea walls.
