## Introduction

Bernard Shaw was an esteemed American journalist who was one of CNN’s main anchors of election night coverage of the 2000 presidential election. The following year, the Edward R. Murrow School of Communications at Washington State University presented him with the Edward R. Murrow Excellence in Journalism award. In his acceptance speech, Shaw confronts the dangers of television news distorting reality, and ultimately speaks out against the horse-race coverage style of live television reporting on November 7, 2000. In this clip, Shaw reflects on his role in what he calls “one of the most egregious acts of news media distortion” and “a journalistic crime against accuracy.” He recounts the pressures of competition and the rush to be first to project a winner on election night, and implores Americans and aspiring journalists to learn from that moment just how important the role of news outlets is to maintain trust and credibility for the sake of democracy.
