## Introduction

In one of the closest presidential elections in American history, Bush became president with only a few hundred total votes in the state of Florida, despite Gore winning the popular vote by a little over 500,000 votes nationwide. The recount efforts prompted analysts and mathematicians to consider what happens when the margins of victory are smaller than the statistical margins of error, and what role technology plays in casting and counting votes. This clip features an accessible discussion of mathematical concepts at play in such processes, as well as a debate about technological interventions like punch-card ballots, the infamous “hanging chads” in Florida recounts, and the fallibility of both humans and machines in the voting process.
